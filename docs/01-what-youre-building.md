# 1. What you're building

## The dream

Picture this: a small robot arm sits on the other side of a chessboard from your
child. She makes her move. The robot thinks for a second, then reaches out,
picks up one of its own pieces, and sets it down — playing a real game with her.
Later on, it can even *talk*: "Nice move! See how that opens up your bishop?"

That's the finished project. It's the kind of patient, encouraging chess partner
that's always available, never gets bored, and plays exactly at her level.

## What it actually looks like

- A **small chessboard** (a travel-size set works great).
- A **little robot arm** clamped to your table on the robot's side.
- Your **computer**, which is the robot's brain.
- The game is **turn-based**: she moves her pieces by hand, the robot moves its
  own pieces with the arm. They take turns, just like two people.

## The honest truth (so you're not surprised)

This is a project you **grow over several weekends**, not something you finish in
an afternoon. The good news: we build it in stages, and *each stage works on its
own*. You'll have a playable chess opponent long before the robot arm is even
involved.

Here's the journey, so you can see where we're headed:

| Stage | What you get | Need a robot? |
|------:|--------------|:-------------:|
| 1 | The pretend arm moves one piece on command | No — just a computer |
| 2 | It knows all the chess rules (captures, castling…) | No |
| 3 | A full game you can play, robot picks its moves | No |
| 4 | The **real arm** moves the pieces | Yes |
| 5 | A camera so it *sees* her move | Yes |
| 6 | A **voice** that talks and coaches | Yes |

## What you need to know going in

**Almost nothing.** You do not need a computer-science background. You need:

- Willingness to **copy and paste** commands and learn a handful of new words
  (we keep a [glossary](09-glossary-and-help.md)).
- **Patience** — when something doesn't work the first time, that's normal, not a
  sign you can't do it.
- A little **curiosity** about how the pieces fit together.

That's it. If you can follow a recipe, you can build this.
