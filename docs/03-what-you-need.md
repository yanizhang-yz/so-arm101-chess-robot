# 3. What you need

Good news first: **for Stages 1–3 you need only a computer.** You can start today
without spending a penny. The shopping below is mostly for later, when you add
the real arm.

## Software (all free)

We install these together on the next page — nothing to do yet:

- **Python** — the language the project is written in. (We use a helper called
  **uv** that installs the right version for you.)
- **The project code** — this very repository, the `chess-robot` folder.
- **Stockfish** — the free chess "brain."

That's everything for Stages 1–3.

## Hardware (for Stage 4 and beyond)

- **A computer** — a Mac is the smoothest. A Windows or Linux PC works too.
- **The robot arm** — this guide is built around the **SO-ARM101** (a popular,
  low-cost, open-source arm used with a free toolkit called *LeRobot*). You want
  the "follower" arm. Roughly a few hundred dollars in parts; sold as kits.
  - It connects to your computer with a **USB cable** and needs its own **power
    supply** (the kind that plugs into the wall).
- **A small chessboard and pieces** — a travel-size set with smallish squares
  (around 4 cm) fits the arm's reach. Pieces with a slim "waist" are easier for
  the gripper to grab. Grab a couple of **spare queens** for promotions.
- **A way to mount things** — a clamp to fix the arm to the table on its side of
  the board.

## Hardware for the *later* stages (Stages 5–6)

- **A USB webcam** on a small stand, looking down at the board (for the "eyes").
- **A microphone and a speaker** (a cheap conference-call puck does both) for the
  "voice."

## Time and money, honestly

- **Money:** $0 to start (Stages 1–3). A few hundred dollars for the arm + small
  bits when you're ready for the real robot.
- **Time:** an evening to get Stages 1–3 running. The real-arm stage is the
  fiddly one — give it a few relaxed weekends, not one rushed night.

## A gentle suggestion

**Do Stages 1–3 first, with just your computer**, before buying anything. You'll
learn how the whole thing works, you'll have fun playing the game, and you'll
*know* you enjoy the project before spending money on the arm.
