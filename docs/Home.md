# Build a Chess-Coaching Robot — A Beginner's Wiki

Welcome! 👋 This is a friendly, step-by-step guide to building a small robot arm
that plays chess with your child — moving its own pieces, playing gently at a
kid's level, and (later) talking and teaching like a coach.

**If you have never written a single line of code, you are in exactly the right
place.** This guide assumes you know nothing about computers beyond opening apps
and typing. We'll explain every word and every command. You can copy and paste
your way through it.

## The one idea to hold onto

> **Build the brain first, and *pretend* the robot until the very end.**

Instead of fighting with a real robot arm from day one, we use a "pretend arm"
that simply prints what it *would* do — *"I'd pick up the piece on e2 and set it
down on e4."* We build the whole chess game with that pretend arm on a regular
computer. Only at the end do we plug in the real arm, and because everything was
already talking to the pretend one, the real arm just slides into its place.

Why? Real hardware is slow and finicky. If you needed the robot plugged in and
working to test every tiny thing, you'd get stuck. So we make the robot the
*last* problem, not the first. **You can start today with just a computer — no
robot needed for the first three stages.**

## A word on safety

This is a robot that moves near a child. It's small and gentle, but please read
the **[safety section](07-step-4-the-real-arm.md#safety-first)** before the arm
ever moves on its own. The golden rule: the robot only moves on its own turn,
slowly, while everyone's hands are clear, with a grown-up watching.

## You can do this

This is a *grow-it-over-weeks* project, not a one-afternoon kit. Every step gives
you a real, working result, so it stays fun the whole way. Let's go. 💛
